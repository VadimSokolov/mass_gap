# Chapter 1. The Problem

This chapter states the Yang Mills mass gap problem and recasts it in the language of probability. It gives the Clay Millennium Prize formulation of Jaffe and Witten: existence of a nontrivial quantum Yang Mills theory on R^4 for a compact simple gauge group with a mass gap Delta > 0. It records the announced 2026 existence proof as a working hypothesis. Via the spectral theorem the gap becomes the left edge of the support of the spectral measure of a gauge invariant observable, so the whole problem is a question about supports of positive measures obtained as limits of lattice measures. The chapter introduces lattice gauge theory as a probability model (Haar measure, plaquette weights, Wilson loops, character expansion, transfer matrix), surveys probabilistic work on gauge theories, and gives a table of the results of the book and their status. It proves nothing new and states that no chapter proves the mass gap.

## From the literature

- Gauge invariance of electromagnetism as a local phase change (Weyl 1929).
- Nonabelian gauge theory, the Yang Mills equations (Yang and Mills 1954).
- Renormalizability of Yang Mills theory ('t Hooft 1971).
- Asymptotic freedom (Gross and Wilczek 1973, Politzer 1973).
- Lattice gauge theory, area law at strong coupling and confinement (Wilson 1974).
- Monte Carlo study of the lattice theory (Creutz 1980); glueball spectrum (Morningstar and Peardon 1999).
- Millennium Prize formulation of the mass gap problem (Jaffe and Witten 2006); Osterwalder Schrader axioms (Osterwalder and Schrader 1973, 1975).
- Construction of phi^4 in three dimensions (Glimm and Jaffe 1987); renormalization group for lattice gauge theory (Balaban 1987, 1988); SU(2) Yang Mills with infrared cutoff (Magnen, Rivasseau and Seneor 1993).

- Compact U(1) in three dimensions, mass gap and confinement (Polyakov 1977, Gopfert and Mack 1982).
- Migdal Kadanoff recursion (Migdal 1975, Kadanoff 1976) and confinement on hierarchical lattices (Ito 1985).
