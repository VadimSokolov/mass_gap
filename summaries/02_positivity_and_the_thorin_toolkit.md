# Chapter 2. Positivity and the Thorin Toolkit

This chapter explains why Euclidean correlations of gauge invariant observables are Laplace transforms of nonnegative measures, and collects the tools used afterwards. Positivity comes from reflection positivity, proved for the lattice Wilson action by Osterwalder and Seiler through nonnegative character coefficients of the plaquette weight (Bochner's theorem on a compact group). Bernstein's theorem makes complete monotonicity of the correlation equivalent to a nonnegative spectral measure, whose support edge is the gap. The chapter defines the offset measure at positive Euclidean time, and proves log convexity of the correlation, monotone decrease of the effective mass to the gap (so numerical estimates only bound the gap from above), and equivalence of a gap and exponential decay. It then reviews harmonic analysis on compact groups (Peter Weyl theorem, Weyl integration and character formulas, Casimirs, Brownian motion, Jacobian of the exponential map) and the probabilistic toolkit: Stieltjes functions, generalized gamma convolutions and Thorin measures, Bondesson's shot noise representation, Levy measures, the hyperbolic laws of Biane, Pitman and Yor, likelihood ratio orders and total positivity, and positive linear inverse problems with EM deconvolution. Most of the material is classical.

## From the literature

- Reflection positivity and the Euclidean axioms (Osterwalder and Schrader 1973, 1975); reflection positivity for lattice gauge theory (Osterwalder and Seiler 1978); selfadjoint strictly positive transfer matrix (Luscher 1977).
- Bochner's theorem on a compact group (Bochner 1933) and the Peter Weyl theorem (Peter and Weyl 1927).
- Bernstein's theorem on completely monotone functions (Bernstein 1929; Widder 1941).
- Infinite divisibility of exponential mixtures (Goldie 1967, Steutel 1967).
- Generalized gamma convolutions and Thorin measures (Thorin 1977); properties and shot noise representation (Bondesson 1992).
- Hyperbolic laws related to Brownian motion and Bessel processes (Biane, Pitman and Yor 2001).
- Monotone likelihood ratio and total positivity (Karlin 1968).
- EM algorithm for positive linear inverse problems (Vardi and Lee 1993); discussion by Polson and Robert (1994).
